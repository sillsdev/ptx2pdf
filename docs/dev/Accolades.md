# Accolades

This document collects a selection of the thanks and feedback we have received
from PTXprint users since the first public releases in 2020. It is hard to blow
our own trumpet, but funders, managers and new users like to see this kind of
thing, and it reminds the team why the work matters.

The quotes below are a small, representative sample. Names, places and language
names have been removed or generalised; edits are shown in [square brackets]
and omissions with an ellipsis (…).

## Why it matters

PTXprint began with a simple conviction: the sooner trial publications get into
the hands of key people in a community, the sooner real Scripture engagement
happens and the sooner teams get vital feedback on the translation. Teams were
being told to wait five to seven months for a trained typesetter to lay out a
single gospel. PTXprint was built so that translators themselves can produce
good-looking print drafts, and increasingly final publications, in minutes.

> I've been asking for YEARS for a simple solution, to allow local
> organizations to format and publish their approved scripture without having to
> send it off to a type-setter. … The [publications department] that some have
> used is 1000s of miles away, requiring us to send piles of large documents back
> and forth through an Internet we don't have. …
>
> Then PTXprint comes along and BOOM, a very accessible solution that gives
> really good results, doable on-location, right away, built directly from the
> actual Paratext files (so no data inaccuracies/inconsistencies between printed
> results and Paratext project files). … PTXprint is probably THE most useful
> tool to come along in years. Literally.
>
> — Language technology worker, Asia

> Thank you so much for building a tool like PTXprint that revolutionized the
> ability to print for people who don't have access to typesetters. This is
> truly a gift … that you have shared with the Bible translation community.
>
> — Translation team member, 2025

> I also am able to do typesetting [remotely] and send PDFs to my team for the
> local printer, using PTXprint, which has been transformational compared to my
> previous time-consuming labor with Microsoft Office.
>
> — Survey respondent, 2025

## In a sentence

said:

- *"You would be surprised at how good a quality book can be made in a short
  time and for no cost."*
- *"Does the heavy lifting in preparing a text to look like it has been crafted
  by a professional."*
- *"A complete process enhancement [that] really empowers the local translation
  teams to get the output they need in order to move the work forward and to get
  good-looking Scripture books into the hands of the people."*

## From the foundations

PTXprint is built on XeTeX and the ptx2pdf macros. On the 1.0 release, the
original author of both wrote:

> Congratulations on this; it's an awesome achievement! It was always my dream
> that something like this could be built on the foundations of XeTeX etc, but
> having the dream isn't the same as bringing it to fruition. So thank you and
> all the team for all the hard work and perseverance.

## Room to grow

Not all feedback is glowing, and the less positive feedback is useful too. Some
users find the range of options overwhelming and stop before they get beyond what
Paratext already gives them. A trainer reported that error messages can be hard
for non-programmers to understand. Even so, the same trainer added that *"the
developers are very easily accessible and very helpful sorting things out."*
Simpler first steps, better error messages and clearer documentation (such as
the new User Manual) remain priorities.
