The Page Filler settings, in plain words

What the page filler is for

When PTXprint lays out a book, some pages come out with one or two empty lines at the bottom of a column. The page filler goes through the book one page at a time and fixes those pages so that every column ends exactly at the bottom margin.

It does not add extra space between lines or paragraphs. Instead it changes the paragraphs themselves, so that some of them take one line more or one line fewer. It has two ways of doing that:

1. Narrower or wider lettersIt can set a paragraph's letters slightly narrower (for example 97%) or slightly wider (103%). Narrower letters fit more words on each line, so the paragraph may lose a line. Wider letters can make it gain one. Your limits are set elsewhere, usually 95% to 105%.
2. Asking for one line more or fewerIt can ask the typesetter to set a paragraph in one line more or fewer than normal, by spacing the words a little more loosely or tightly. This is TeX's "looseness".

In these settings, shrink means "make a paragraph take fewer lines" and stretch means "make it take more".

The one idea you need

How it chooses: badness points

For every possible change to every paragraph, the filler first measures how the result looks and gives it badness points. A change that hardly shows gets very few points. A change that looks ugly gets many points.

Then, for each short page, it looks for the combination of changes with the fewest total points that makes the page full. It tries that combination for real, checks the page, and if it does not work, tries the next cheapest.

Think of it as shopping on a budget: every change has a price, and the filler buys the cheapest set of changes that does the job. The settings fall into two kinds:

Speed settingsHow long and how hard the filler searches. They don't change what a good page looks like. They change how often it finds one.
  • Time limit for the whole job
  • Earlier pages to retry
  • Paragraphs to adjust on each page
Quality settingsHow the points are counted, so which changes the filler sees as cheap and which as expensive. One of them is a hard rule.
  • Widest word space allowed (the hard rule)
  • Keep paragraphs close to normal width
  • Penalise shrink or stretch
  • Keep neighbouring paragraphs alike
Diglot & polyglot onlyIgnored for a single-language book. They control how the columns of different languages line up.
  • The cost of imbalance in a polyglot slice
  • Effort to narrow a big imbalance
  • Most tries to fill a polyglot page
  • Fill polyglot slices regardless of 1 line gaps

In the PTXprint panel, a setting's label is shown in bold when you have changed it from its default value.

Speed settings

How hard it tries

Every attempt is a real typesetting run of the book (or the part being worked on), so each attempt takes time. These three settings trade time for fewer failed pages.

Time limit for the whole job (minutes)

default 180range 0–1000

A stopwatch for the whole print job. The filler works on all the books at the same time. When the time runs out, it stops. Any book that isn't finished keeps the pages already filled, and the rest of that book is left as it was. Usually only the longest book is affected.

Set it to 0 for no time limit.

Turn it upif your longest book (Psalms, Isaiah, Jeremiah…) is cut off before the end.
Turn it downif you only want a quick draft and don't mind some unfilled pages.

Earlier pages to retry

default 4range 0–10

Sometimes a page simply can't be filled, because of where it starts. The filler can then go back to the page before, lay it out a different way so that the problem page starts at a different place, and try again. This setting is how many pages it may go back.

Turn it upif you get many failed pages. Fewer pages fail, but the job can become much slower.
Turn it downfor speed. 0 means never go back (fastest, most failures).

In diglot and polyglot books the full number is used only until the first page that fails; after that the filler goes back just one page.

Paragraphs to adjust on each page

default 6range 1–10

The most paragraphs on one page that the filler may change at the same time. Each poetry line (\q1, \q2…) counts as a paragraph of its own, so poetry pages have many "paragraphs".

Turn it upif pages fail even though there seem to be plenty of paragraphs that could change. More pages can be filled, but each page takes longer.
Turn it downto go faster, or to make the filler spread its changes over fewer paragraphs.
Quality settings

How good the text must look

These settings decide what the filler is allowed to do, and which changes it prefers. The first is a hard rule. The other three only change the points, so they move the filler's preferences without forbidding anything.

Widest word space allowed (× text size)

default 1.0range 0–10

The one hard rule. After a change, the filler measures the average gap between words in the paragraph. If it's wider than this, the change is thrown away and never used, however useful it would be.

The number is a fraction of the text size. At 10 pt text, 0.3 means an average word space of 3 pt, and 1.0 means a space as wide as the letter "M". A normal word space is about 0.3 to 0.5.

Turn it upif many pages fail. The filler gets more ways to fill pages, but the text may look spread out.
Turn it downfor tighter, more even text. Pages become harder to fill and the job takes longer.

This is the setting most likely to cause failed pages. Too low and the filler is almost never allowed to stretch a paragraph, so the only remaining option is to shrink one.

Keep paragraphs close to normal width

default 1.0range 0–10

How strongly the filler prefers small changes in letter width over big ones. A paragraph at 99% hardly shows; one at 95% can look cramped. This setting adds points for distance from 100%, so a paragraph that can lose a line at 99% is chosen ahead of one that needs 95%.

Turn it upif you see paragraphs that look obviously squashed or stretched. The filler will spread the work into many small changes.
Turn it downif you'd rather have one paragraph change a lot than several paragraphs change a little.

Penalise shrink or stretch

default 1.0range −5 to +5

When the filler could either make letters narrower or make them wider, which should it prefer? This setting adds points to one direction only.

How strong is "strong"? A paragraph's normal score is roughly its average word space, about 0.3 points. At the default of 1.0, letters at 105% add 0.5 points. At 3.5 they add about 1.75, roughly six times a normal paragraph's score. That makes wider letters a last resort.

Move it up (positive)if wider letters look worse in your script than narrower ones.
Move it down (negative)if your font looks cramped when narrowed and you'd rather it spread out.

Which one looks worse depends on the font and the script. Print a page with a few adjusted paragraphs and look at them.

Keep neighbouring paragraphs alike

default 3range 0–20

The filler compares each paragraph with the one just above it. A narrow paragraph sitting right next to a wide one is easy to spot, because one looks darker than the other. This setting adds points for the difference, so the page looks even.

Turn it upif you see "light" and "dark" paragraphs next to each other.
Turn it downif pages fail and you can accept less even colour.
Diglot & polyglot only

Lining up the languages

In a diglot or polyglot, each language (each glot) has its own column. The text is cut into slices, chunks such as a few verses, which start level across all the columns. The next slice starts below whichever language ran longest.

Since one language is usually longer than another, the shorter ones leave some white space at the end of each slice. That white space is the imbalance.

The filler can stretch or shrink the paragraphs of a short glot to close its gap. Normally it only does that if the glot then ends exactly level with the slice, and it never leaves a gap of exactly one line. You can change that with the last setting below.

The cost of imbalance in a polyglot slice

default 1.0range 0–10

How bad it is for a slice not to line up. With the default of 1.0, the first line of white space costs 10 points. A good paragraph adjustment costs under about 2 points, so the filler will happily make several small adjustments to level one slice.

Turn it upif levelled slices matter more to you than perfectly even paragraphs.
Turn it downif the filler is distorting paragraphs just to tidy up slice endings.

Effort to narrow a big imbalance

default 0.5range 0–10

The cost of each extra line of white space after the first. It decides how much worse a 5-line gap is than a 2-line gap. It doesn't narrow gaps by itself. It helps the filler choose between layouts that leave different amounts of white space in the glots that can't be made level.

Turn it upif you see large white holes in one language's column and want fewer of them, even at the cost of looser paragraphs.
Turn it down (0 = any gap counts as one line) if you'd rather keep paragraphs untouched and accept big gaps.

Most tries to fill a polyglot page

default 20range 0–500

The most layout runs the filler spends on one page. When it goes back to re-do an earlier page for it, each earlier page gets half as many tries. A page that can be filled usually is within a few tries, so this mainly limits time wasted on pages that can't be.

A page where at least one language reaches the bottom is kept as the best layout found, not counted as a failure. 0 means no limit.

Turn it upif a few pages are left short and you have time to spare.
Turn it downto finish faster.

Fill polyglot slices regardless of 1 line gaps

default off

Normally the filler changes a short glot only if that makes it end level with the slice, and it never leaves a gap of exactly one line, which looks like a mistake. Tick this box to let it narrow gaps part of the way, even down to one line.

Recipes

What to change for common problems

Change one or two settings at a time, run the job again, and compare. Return to the defaults if things get confusing.

"Too many pages fail"

  • Raise Widest word space allowed to 0.5–0.6.
  • Raise Earlier pages to retry to 4–6.
  • Raise Paragraphs to adjust to 7–8.
  • Lower Keep neighbouring paragraphs alike a little.

"It takes far too long"

  • Lower Earlier pages to retry to 1–2.
  • Lower Paragraphs to adjust to 4–5.
  • Polyglot: lower Most tries to 10.
  • Set a sensible Time limit.

"Some paragraphs look squashed or gappy"

  • Lower Widest word space allowed to about 0.4.
  • Raise Keep paragraphs close to normal width to 2–3.
  • Raise Keep neighbouring paragraphs alike.
  • Expect more failed pages; you may need to retry more pages.

"Narrow letters look bad in my font"

  • Set Penalise shrink or stretch to 0 or a negative number such as −1.
  • Give stretching room: Widest word space allowed 0.5 or more.

"Big white holes in one language"

  • Raise Effort to narrow a big imbalance to 1 or more.
  • If you can live with partial gaps, tick Fill polyglot slices regardless.

"I don't know where to start"

  • Use the defaults.
  • Change only Widest word space allowed (quality versus failures) and Earlier pages to retry (speed).
  • The rest are fine-tuning.
