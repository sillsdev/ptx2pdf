Audience: Bible translators and typesetters who already know common English computing words (font, margin, PDF, file). The Korean is there to help them read the screen faster, not to be a literary translation.

- Language: standard South Korean, Hangul only (no Hanja). Use the computing terms Korean software normally uses (파일, 폴더, 글꼴, 설정, 탭, 미리 보기).
- Register: polite and formal. Statements end in 합니다체 (-합니다, -됩니다). Instructions use -하세요 / -세요 (선택하세요, 입력하세요). Do not use 반말 and do not address the user as 당신.
- Labels and buttons: short noun phrases, or the noun form of the verb (저장, 취소, 적용, 찾아보기). Tooltips and messages: complete, natural sentences. Translate the meaning, never word by word (see the term notes); if a literal rendering reads as nonsense, rethink it.
- Do not translate product and technical names: Paratext, PTXprint, USFM, FRT, XeTeX, ptx2pdf, and any code such as \k or \w. Keep PDF and similar acronyms in Latin script. Strong's stays a name: 스트롱 번호 (Strong's numbers), never a word for strength.
- Bible terms use the standard Korean Bible vocabulary: 성경, 책 or 권 (book), 장 (chapter), 절 (verse), 관주 (Bible cross-references).
- Typesetting terms: 테두리 (border), 여백 (margin), 각주 (footnote), 머리글 (header), 바닥글 (footer), 단 (column), 책등 (spine), 재단 여유 (bleed), 제본 여백 (gutter), 홀수/짝수 쪽 (odd/even pages).
- Strings containing markup such as <span ...> or <b>: translate only the visible words; leave the tags and attributes exactly as they are.
- Every translation must keep each placeholder ({}, %s, %(name)s), tag and blank-line paragraph break exactly as in the English. A placeholder is a whole unit: never put Korean inside or attached to the braces.
