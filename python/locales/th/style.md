Audience: Bible translators and typesetters who already know common English computing words (font, margin, PDF, file). The Thai is there to help them read the screen faster, not to be a literary translation.

- Register: polite and neutral. Do not use pronouns such as "คุณ", "ท่าน", "มัน" or "พวกมัน"; write instructions as bare verb phrases (เลือก…, คลิก…, ป้อน…). Do not add particles such as ครับ/ค่ะ/นะ. Never write "ส่งมันทาง…"; just say "ส่งทาง…".
- Spelling: always use the precomposed sara am "ำ" (U+0E33), never the separate nikhahit + sara aa "ํา" (U+0E4D U+0E32). Do not insert spaces between words inside a phrase; use a single space only between phrases or clauses where Thai readers expect a break.
- Labels and buttons: a short verb phrase or noun phrase (เปิดไฟล์, ขนาดตัวอักษร). Tooltips and messages: full sentences, plain and natural, not a word-by-word translation of the English. Never translate English idioms literally: if a draft reads as nonsense (e.g. "Embolden" as "มีความกล้า"), re-think the meaning.
- Technical words with no everyday Thai equivalent: transliterate into Thai script in the common form (ไฟล์, โฟลเดอร์, ฟอนต์, แท็บ, อีเมล). Keep PDF, XML, URL and similar acronyms in Latin script.
- Do not translate product and technical names: Paratext, PTXprint, USFM, FRT, XeTeX, ptx2pdf, and any code such as \k or \w.
- Bible terms use standard Thai Bible vocabulary: บท (chapter), ข้อ (verse), หนังสือ (book), พระคัมภีร์ (Scripture), พระคัมภีร์ไบเบิล (Bible).
- Use Arabic numerals. Keep ":" for labels that end in a colon in English.
